# Emergency Procurement Procedure

## 1. Definition

### 1.1 Emergency

An emergency is an event that threatens safety, causes a critical service outage, or creates a legal deadline that cannot be met through the normal procedure.

### 1.2 Not an emergency

A late request, poor planning, or a routine laptop refresh is not an emergency. Price urgency alone does not justify emergency procurement.

### 1.3 Declaration

The procurement manager must declare the emergency in writing before execution, unless immediate action is required to prevent harm.

## 2. Emergency threshold

### 2.1 Value limit

The emergency procedure may be used up to INR 2,00,000 per event. A higher value requires finance director approval before execution.

### 2.2 Single quotation

One quotation is acceptable when a second supplier cannot respond in time. The reason for the single quotation must be recorded.

### 2.3 Supplier restriction

A suspended supplier cannot be used under the emergency procedure. An inactive supplier requires administrator approval and a documented reactivation check.

## 3. Required evidence

### 3.1 Emergency record

The record must state the event, the harm being prevented, the time of declaration, the product, quantity, supplier, price, and expected delivery.

### 3.2 Certification check

`ISO9001` is still required. If the certification cannot be verified before execution, the procurement manager must record the gap and complete verification within 5 business days.

### 3.3 Price check

The buyer must compare the price with the last known price or a public reference where available. A price more than 25 percent above the reference requires a written justification.

## 4. Post-facto approval

### 4.1 Deadline

Post-facto approval must be recorded within 2 business days of the emergency purchase.

### 4.2 Approver

The normal value-band approver reviews the emergency record. If that approver is unavailable, the next level approves.

### 4.3 Rejection

If the post-facto review rejects the purchase, the procurement manager must record corrective action and escalate to finance.

## 5. Delivery and warranty

### 5.1 Delivery

The emergency procedure may accept delivery above 45 days only when the emergency record explains why the delay is unavoidable.

### 5.2 Warranty

The standard warranty in POL-006 still applies. A shorter warranty requires a written exception approved by the procurement manager.

### 5.3 Receipt

A second person must verify receipt and condition when the value exceeds INR 50,000.

## 6. Audit

### 6.1 Audit event

Every emergency declaration, approval, and exception generates an audit event with a correlation ID.

### 6.2 Review

Procurement reviews emergency purchases monthly to identify repeated use, missing evidence, and supplier performance issues.

### 6.3 Misuse

Repeated use of the emergency procedure for routine purchases may result in removal of emergency authority.
