# Supplier Code of Conduct

## 1. Principles

### 1.1 Lawful conduct

Suppliers must comply with applicable laws on anti-bribery, competition, labour, health and safety, and data protection.

### 1.2 Integrity

Suppliers must not offer or accept improper payments, gifts, hospitality, or other advantages intended to influence a procurement decision.

### 1.3 Honest information

Quotations, certifications, availability statements, and delivery commitments must be accurate. Material errors must be disclosed promptly.

## 2. Conflicts of interest

### 2.1 Disclosure

A supplier must disclose any relationship between its owners or employees and an employee involved in the procurement decision.

### 2.2 Recusal

An employee with a disclosed conflict must not participate in evaluation or approval. The conflict and recusal are recorded in the audit trail.

### 2.3 Gifts

Gifts and hospitality above INR 5,000 in value must be declined or declared to the procurement owner. Cash or cash equivalents are never permitted.

## 3. Labour and human rights

### 3.1 Prohibited labour

Suppliers must not use forced, bonded, child, or trafficked labour. Workers must receive legally required wages and safe working conditions.

### 3.2 Non-discrimination

Suppliers must not discriminate on the basis of protected characteristics and must provide a route for workers to raise concerns.

### 3.3 Subcontractors

Suppliers remain responsible for the conduct of subcontractors used to fulfil an order.

## 4. Environmental responsibility

### 4.1 Compliance

Suppliers must hold environmental permits required for their operations and must manage hazardous materials lawfully.

### 4.2 Waste and packaging

Suppliers should minimise unnecessary packaging and provide take-back or recycling options where practical.

### 4.3 Product information

Suppliers must provide accurate material and safety information needed for lawful use and disposal.

## 5. Information handling

### 5.1 Confidential information

Suppliers must protect confidential organisational information, use it only for the agreed purpose, and return or delete it when the engagement ends.

### 5.2 Security incidents

A suspected loss or unauthorised disclosure of organisational data must be reported within 24 hours of discovery.

### 5.3 Subprocessors

A supplier must not disclose organisational data to a subprocessor without written authorisation and an equivalent data protection obligation.

## 6. Reporting and consequences

### 6.1 Reporting channel

Concerns may be reported to the procurement owner or the compliance contact. Reports are handled confidentially and without retaliation.

### 6.2 Investigation

Procurement will investigate credible reports and may request records, interviews, or corrective action plans.

### 6.3 Remediation

A supplier may be suspended while a serious breach is investigated. Repeated or unremediated breaches may result in inactivation or contract termination.

### 6.4 Record retention

Investigation records are retained for audit. Supplier identity and status changes remain in PostgreSQL as the system of record.
